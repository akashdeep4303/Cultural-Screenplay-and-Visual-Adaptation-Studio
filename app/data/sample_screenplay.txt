THE UNOPENED LETTER
A short family drama in four scenes.

Written for cultural adaptation (source is culturally neutral).


SCENE 1
EXT. HOUSE FRONT YARD - EARLY MORNING

A modest two-storey house. Dew on the grass. A metal gate that never quite closes. ADAM (28), restless, stands with an unopened envelope. He has not slept.

MOTHER (55) watches from the doorway, a shawl around her shoulders. SISTER (22) sits on the low wall, pretending to check her phone.

                    MOTHER
          You came back late. You always do this
          when something is wrong.

                    ADAM
          Nothing is wrong. I just needed air.

He turns the envelope over. The handwriting is his uncle's.

                    SISTER
          That letter has been in your pocket for
          two days. Either open it or give it to her.

                    MOTHER
          If it is from my brother, it is a family
          matter. Do not hide family matters from me.

Adam does not answer. He puts the letter back in his jacket pocket and walks toward the kitchen door. Mother's worry stays on her face. Sister follows him with her eyes, then looks down.


SCENE 2
INT. KITCHEN - MORNING

Steam from a kettle. Three cups wait on the table. The kitchen is the true centre of the house. FRIEND (27) has arrived early, as he always does when Adam is in trouble. He leans against the counter, trying to keep the mood light.

                    FRIEND
          You look like a man who lost a bet and
          then lost the walk home.

                    ADAM
          My uncle wrote. He wants me to take the
          shop. Mother still thinks I am studying.

                    SISTER
          You told her you had one year left.

                    MOTHER
          I can hear you. I am not a guest in my
          own kitchen.

Silence. Adam's hand stays on the pocket that holds the letter. The kettle clicks off.

                    MOTHER
          Sit. Drink. Then tell me the truth, not
          the version you prepared for strangers.

Adam sits. He does not take the letter out. Sister places a cup in front of him, then one in front of Mother. Friend looks at the floor, suddenly not funny.

                    ADAM
          After work. I will tell you after work.

                    MOTHER
          After work is how lies grow old.

Adam stands, still carrying the letter, and leaves through the back door. Sister starts to speak, then stops.


SCENE 3
EXT. MARKET STREET - AFTERNOON

A busy street of small shops, buses, and heat. Adam walks with Friend. The letter is still in his pocket. A hawker calls out. A bicycle bell cuts through the noise.

                    FRIEND
          If you take the shop, you stay. If you
          stay, you become the man she already
          introduced to the neighbours.

                    ADAM
          If I refuse, she will think I am ashamed
          of her brother's work.

                    FRIEND
          Then do not refuse in the street. Go home
          and let her see your face when you say it.

They stop near a tea stall. Adam almost opens the envelope, then puts it away again.

                    FRIEND
          Your sister already knows. Your mother
          almost knows. The only person still
          pretending is you.

                    ADAM
          I will go home before evening. I will not
          bring a stranger's courage with me.

He keeps the letter. They part. The market continues without noticing.


SCENE 4
INT. LIVING ROOM - EVENING

The house is quieter. A single lamp. Mother sits in the main chair. Sister stands near the window. Adam enters, dusty from the street, the same jacket, the same letter.

He takes the envelope out and places it on the low table between them. He does not sit until Mother nods.

                    ADAM
          Uncle asked me to leave the course and
          take the shop. I hid the letter because
          I was afraid of disappointing you.

                    MOTHER
          I can survive disappointment. I cannot
          survive being the last to know.

                    SISTER
          We would have sat with you. That is the
          whole point of this house.

                    ADAM
          I am sorry. I am still not sure I want
          the shop. I am sure I should have said so.

Mother picks up the letter, feels its weight, and does not open it yet. She looks at her son, then at her daughter.

                    MOTHER
          Then we will read it together. After that,
          you will tell me what you want, not what
          you think I can bear.

Sister sits. Adam sits. The letter stays on the table, no longer hidden, not yet finished.

END.
